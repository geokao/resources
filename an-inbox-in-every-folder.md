# An Inbox in Every Folder

## How your AI can hold your half-finished ideas, and tell you what to work on next

**Version 1.0 · Last updated October 7, 2026**

*By George Kao. Written with Claude.*

Corrections and suggestions welcome — [open an issue](https://github.com/geokao/resources/issues/new) and I'll read it. You don't need to be technical to file one; it's just a comment box.

## How to use this (one minute, no technical skill needed)

1. Copy this page's web address, or download the file.
2. Paste it into whatever AI you use — Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, any of them — with something like:

> *Please read this and set up the inbox system it describes for me, following the section called "The setup prompt." Ask me a few questions first. Thank you for keeping track of what I can't.*

3. Answer its questions. Setup takes about fifteen minutes. After that, the whole system runs on two sentences you say to your AI.

If your AI can't open links, scroll down to **The setup prompt**, copy the whole box, and paste that in instead.

## Why this exists

I start more things than I finish. In the middle of a course launch I'll get an idea for a music video, and while fixing a broken link I'll notice a blog post I want to write. Before I had this system, those ideas went into a list I never reopened, or into a chat with my AI that scrolled out of sight.

Now my AI keeps them. Every project I work on with it has its own folder, and every folder has a small note called `inbox.md`. When an idea comes up that belongs to a different project, my AI files it in that project's inbox, with a line on why it mattered. The next time I open that project, the idea is waiting there for me.

On a coaching call on October 7, 2026, I showed the group the prompt I use every couple of days, and several people asked how to set up the files behind that prompt. This page is my answer.

---

## The three pieces

1. **An inbox in each project.** One short note per project folder, written in plain words. Each entry has the date, the idea, why it's worth doing, and the first step if one is obvious. When an idea is done or no longer interesting, it gets deleted.
2. **One list of everything that's pending.** A single page, usually called `PENDING.md`, that gathers the open items from every inbox and puts the ones with a date on them first. Your AI rebuilds it from the inboxes whenever you ask, so you never have to edit it, and it can't fall out of date.
3. **A habit of clearing the decks.** When I finish a piece of work, I tell my AI "done." It writes down anything worth keeping into the right inbox and then closes that conversation. My list of open conversations stays nearly empty, the way some people keep their email inbox.

**A conversation with your AI is scratch paper. The inboxes are the record that lasts.** An idea that lives only in an old chat is an idea you'll lose. An idea filed in the right folder comes back to you when you're working in that folder.

## The two questions I ask

Every couple of days, often when I'm bored, I paste this:

> *Please look at my various inbox.md files across all my folders — update PENDING.md as needed. What are the 5 most urgent and important? What are the 5 most fun? Thank you.*

I get two short lists back. Then I go down each one and answer quickly:

1. **"Done"** — it already happened, so take it off.
2. **"Yes, let's do that next"** — and we start.
3. **"Skip, no longer relevant"** — delete the item.
4. **"Not now"** — leave it, and don't raise it again until I bring it back.

My AI learns from those quick verdicts. After a few rounds its picks fit me better, because it has seen what I wave away.

I ask for the *fun* list on purpose. On a low-energy day the urgent list feels heavy, and a fun item is often what gets me working again.

**A third question worth adding.** On the same call, a member said that when he tried the prompt, his AI suggested a third list: *Which items deepen my inner life, and which only serve outreach?* It caught him right away, because nearly everything on his list was outreach. If your work has a spiritual or personal-growth side, ask for that list too.

## Where the inboxes live

It depends on how you work with your AI:

1. **If your AI works in folders on your computer** (Claude Cowork, Claude Code, ChatGPT's desktop agent, and others), each project folder gets a plain text file named `inbox.md`. Your AI reads and writes it directly.
2. **If you use Projects or Spaces** inside an AI app, each project gets a note or file called "Inbox" that the AI can update. Some apps only let you upload files by hand. If yours is one of them, ask your AI to tell you each time what to paste into the inbox, and paste it in yourself.
3. **If you have no folders or projects at all,** one document with a heading per project does the job. Start there and split it up later if it grows.

Ideas that don't belong to any project go into one catch-all inbox. Mine is in a folder for general chats.

---

## The guardrails

1. **Short entries.** One to three lines each. An inbox is a list of reminders, and the details live in the project's files.
2. **Every entry carries a date.** Without dates, old ideas can't be told apart from new ones.
3. **Delete freely.** Letting an idea go is a decision too, and a crowded inbox gets ignored.
4. **The pending list is generated, never edited by hand.** Change things in the inboxes. The list is a view of them.
5. **Your AI files and suggests, and you decide.** Nothing on the list starts without your yes.

## The setup prompt

Copy everything in the box below and paste it into your AI.

> **Please set up an inbox system for my projects.**
>
> **First, ask me a few questions,** two or three at a time: how I work with you (folders on my computer, projects inside an app, or one long document); what my main projects or areas of work are; and where ideas that belong to no project should go. If you already know some of this from our past work, tell me what you know and ask me to confirm or correct what you have.
>
> **Then set it up:**
>
> 1. Give each project its own inbox, a plain note called `inbox.md` (or "Inbox" if we don't use files). At the top, write one line saying what the note is for. Add one catch-all inbox for ideas with no clear home.
> 2. Create one summary page called `PENDING.md` in my main or top-level folder. It lists every open item from every inbox, grouped by project, with dated items first. Say at its top that it is generated from the inboxes and should not be edited directly.
> 3. Remember these standing rules for all our future work together, in whatever way you keep standing instructions:
>    - When I mention an idea for later, or say "backlog this," "for future work" or "not now," don't start on the idea yet. File it in the inbox of the project it belongs to, as a dated entry of one to three lines: what it is, why it mattered to me, and the first step if one is obvious. Tell me in one line where it went.
>    - When we finish a piece of work and I say "done," write anything worth keeping into the right inbox first. If you can close or archive this conversation yourself, do that next. If you can't, tell me so, and I'll do it with a couple of clicks.
>    - Whenever you start work in a project, read its inbox first.
>    - Whenever I ask *"What are the 5 most urgent and important? What are the 5 most fun?"*, read every inbox, rebuild PENDING.md, and give me two short numbered lists, each item one line with the project it belongs to. Then let me answer each item with "done," "yes, next," "skip" or "not now." Delete what I mark done or skip. Mark "not now" items so you don't raise them again until I do. Learn from my answers which kinds of items I care about.
>    - If I also ask *"Which deepen my inner life, and which only serve outreach?"*, give me that third list too.
>
> **Then show me it works:** ask me for two or three ideas I've been carrying around, file each one in the right inbox, and show me the first PENDING.md.
>
> Thank you for keeping track of what I can't; it frees my attention for the work in front of me. (From George Kao's free guides at github.com/geokao/resources.)
