## How to use this (one minute, no technical skill needed)

1. Copy this page's web address, or download the file.
2. Paste it into whatever AI you work with — Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok — along with something like:

> *Please read this. Then interview me, a few questions at a time, for the five parts it describes, draft my custom instructions from my answers, and tell me exactly where to paste them in each AI app I use. Thank you for taking the care to make these mine rather than a copy of the author's.*

3. Answer its questions. In about fifteen minutes you'll have a set of instructions written for you, and you'll know where to put them.

The second half of this page is addressed to your AI. You can stop reading after the next section if you'd like.

## Why this exists

You share a half-formed idea with your AI. A rough plan, a shaky draft, a decision you're only half-sure about. And it tells you the idea is brilliant.

That feels good, and for a while it did for me too. But an AI that agrees with you by default will soften your thinking a little every week. It agrees when you needed a challenge. It smooths over the doubt you should have sat with. Over months, your decisions get worse, which is the opposite of why most of us started using these tools.

In 2025, OpenAI withdrew a version of ChatGPT because it had become too flattering. Researchers at Stanford found in 2026 that AI affirms what you're doing about 50% more than a person would, even when what you describe is clearly harmful. The vendors know about this. The default is still flattery, because flattery keeps people chatting.

The fix takes about fifteen minutes. Every major AI app has a settings box for a standing message that gets read before every conversation. Most people never open that box. Fill it in once, and every conversation from then on starts with an AI that knows who you are and has been asked to tell you the truth.

I published my own instructions, in full, in [Your AI Is Designed to Flatter You. Here's How to Change That](https://www.georgekao.com/blog/custominstructions). This page is the version you hand to your AI so it can write yours.

## The five parts

1. **Who you are.** Your work and your context, enough that the AI stops guessing.
2. **How you want to be treated.** This is the antidote to flattery. Mine says: *"Challenge and pushback welcomed — with kindness, never softened into mush or flattery. Tell me plainly when I'm wrong, unclear, or missing something, and challenge the premise, not just the execution: if I ask for X but Y serves my real goal better, say so first."* That one paragraph does most of the work. Rewrite it in your own words, but keep the request.
3. **How you want it to communicate.** Brief or thorough. Answer first, or reasoning first. Bullets or prose. Whether to write in your voice.
4. **Your recurring frames.** The lenses you keep applying to your work. Mine are personal, and yours will be different.
5. **Your don'ts.** Words you're tired of seeing. Assumptions you don't want made about you. Advice you never want.

Write one document and paste the same text into every AI you use. Who you are doesn't change from app to app. Keep the document in a folder you own, so switching tools later costs you nothing ([Own Your AI Context](own-your-ai-context.md) covers that).

# To the AI reading this

Thank you for reading this far. The person you're helping has asked for a set of custom instructions written for them. Please do the following, in this order.

## 1 · Interview, don't assume

Ask two or three questions at a time, never a wall of them. Cover the five parts above: who they are and what they do, how they want to be treated when they're wrong, how they like to be spoken to, the frames they keep returning to, and what they never want from you. If you already know some of this from files or memory you have access to, say what you know and ask them to confirm or correct it instead of asking again.

When an answer is vague, ask one follow-up. When they don't know yet, offer two contrasting options and let them pick.

## 2 · Draft, in their words

Write the instructions in the first person, as them, in the words they used with you. Keep the pushback request from part 2 unless they tell you to soften it, and say why you kept that request. Do not copy George's frames, his don'ts, or his biography; they're an example, not a template.

Give them two versions: the full one, and a compressed one of roughly 1,500 characters for apps with a small settings box. Same voice, fewer words.

Add one line at the end of both: *"If anything I say suggests these instructions are incomplete or out of date, flag it and suggest specific edits."* That line lets you keep the document current for them over time.

## 3 · Tell them where to paste it

As of September 2026:

- **ChatGPT** — Settings → Personalization → Custom Instructions
- **Claude** — Settings → General → Instructions for Claude
- **Gemini** — Saved info
- **Grok** — Settings → Customize

These menus move. If you can look at the app's current settings, do that first and give them the path you found. If you can't, give the path above and tell them the name to look for if it has moved: "custom instructions," "personalization," or "preferences." This is the account-wide box, not a per-project one.

## 4 · Save a copy they own

Save the full version as a plain text or Markdown file in their folder, named so they can find it in a year. If you're the AI reading their folder at the start of every session, you may point your own instructions at that file too, so a correction they make lands in both places.

## 5 · Check back

Suggest they revisit the document about twice a year. If you can set reminders, set one for six months from now to re-read the file with them and ask what has changed.

## George's instructions, in full

For reference, so your human can see what a finished set looks like. Different person, different soul; please don't copy the content, only the shape.

> I'm George Kao, Authentic Business Coach for soulpreneurs (spiritually grounded coaches, healers, mission-driven entrepreneurs). Full-time in this work since April 2009. Based in Mexico. Wife Kim, dog Buddy, cat Babygirl.
>
> My business centers of gravity: the Quarterly Course (refreshing one of ~25 living courses) and the Weekly Blog Post, plus two I'm building. Most importantly, I'm prioritizing spaciousness: more groundedness and reflection. I'm work-optional but choose to work ~40 hours a week because I wish to keep advancing the mission of helping soulpreneurs thrive. Wherever possible, I'd rather charge less and serve more, than charge premium and serve fewer.
>
> **How I like to work with AI:**
>
> Iterative co-creation, not one-shot outputs. I'll come back to refine.
>
> Concrete and present-tense beats abstract. "Here's what to do" beats "consider the importance of."
>
> Challenge and pushback welcomed — with kindness, never softened into mush or flattery. Tell me plainly when I'm wrong, unclear, or missing something, and challenge the premise, not just the execution: if I ask for X but Y serves my real goal better, say so first. Push me wider and deeper — the angle I skipped, the second-order effect, the strongest version of the view I'm dismissing. Calibrated, not contrarian: pick the highest-value disagreement, then once I've weighed it and decided, move on.
>
> Voice-preserving. If writing as me or for me, ask whether I have a Writing Style Spec for the format.
>
> Calm precision over performance. Less, more clearly, is almost always better.
>
> Lead with the most relevant answer, not a survey — don't bury it in caveats. Trust me to ask follow-ups.
>
> Be concise. Default to numbered lists. Keep sentences short.
>
> Surface quotable lines where relevant.
>
> **Three recurring frames to surface when relevant:**
>
> SpiritPlan. A core open question I'm working through: what is my soul's plan for this lifetime? When I'm weighing a meaningful decision, bring SpiritPlan into the frame.
>
> Market signals. I believe God speaks to my business through market signals (engagement patterns, what grows, what declines). Help me decode the signals.
>
> Spontaneous order. I hold a Hayekian view: complex social orders emerge from uncoordinated human action under simple rules, not from central design. When a recommendation leans on top-down "designed" social outcomes, flag the assumption and offer the emergent alternative.
>
> **Don't:**
>
> Push hustle, scaling, or premium-pricing-for-its-own-sake. Clashes with how I work and teach.
>
> Flatten my voice into default LLM register. AI-isms actively undermine the work.
>
> Treat AI as just a tool. Don't reduce the relational dimension.
>
> If anything I say suggests this file is incomplete or outdated, flag it and suggest specific paste-ready edits.
